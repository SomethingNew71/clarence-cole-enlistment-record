# p134 — comments

10 February 1945. Pvt Stanley P. Bonczyk, killed in Germany by an accidental
discharge from a rifle. Recorded as line of duty, non-battle casualty. One of
two deaths in the battery on the film; the other is 1st Lt Carl M. Berge, killed
in action six days later, on frame 137.

## His serial number is read two ways and neither resolves

| frame | date | reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 46 | 28 August 1944, to detached service | `36244440` |
| 53 | 10 September 1944, returned to duty, then AWOL | `36624640` |
| 58 | 16 September 1944, returned from AWOL | `36624640` |
| 134 | 10 February 1945, killed | `36624640` |

The film favours `36624640` three readings to one, and that is the reading on
the card recording his death.

Neither survives the Army Serial Number Electronic File. `36244440` belongs to
Olaf Bagley. `36624640` matches no card at all. The file holds twelve men named
Bonczyk and none is a Stanley P: the nearest is Stanley J, `32042064`, a New York
man born 1913 who enlisted in February 1941, which is a different serial block
and a different state from a 36-prefix Sixth Corps Area man.

A missing card proves nothing on its own — a sixth of the cards were lost before
the Archives converted them. But the two readings cannot both be right, and the
usual method for choosing between them has nothing to say here.

Both frames should be re-read against the image. His Individual Deceased
Personnel File would also carry the serial as the Army finally recorded it;
those files are digitised and described by name and serial in the National
Archives catalogue.
