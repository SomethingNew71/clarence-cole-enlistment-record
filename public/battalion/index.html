The men

The Battalion, by the numbers

Battery C carried about a hundred and twenty men. The morning report counted them every day and recorded every change in any one of them. Two orders in the autumn of 1945 then replaced most of the battalion around it.

Strength, day by day

Enlisted men only, as the card records them: how many were on the roll, and how many were present for duty. The gap between the lines is the men who were not — in hospital, on leave, detached to another unit. The clerk stopped writing the strength line on 12 July 1945, so the chart ends there rather than at the end of the film.

What happened to the men

Every status change the clerk wrote on a card, sorted by the words he used. These are entries, not men: a man who went to hospital and came back is counted twice. Hover a bar to see one of the entries behind it, in his wording.

568 entries across 495 cards. Two could not be sorted and are shown as such.

Wounded and killed

Seven men wounded or injured, and two killed. That is all the film records, quoted as the clerk wrote it. Two of the entries are corrections filed weeks later, and carry the date of the day they describe rather than the card they appear on.

The two orders

Two orders, three weeks apart, replaced most of the battalion. On 24 August 1945 Special Orders 66 transferred 142 men to the 70th Infantry Division for return to the United States. On 11 September the 29th Infantry Division, which was also going home, assigned 241 of its men to the 153rd. The two orders name 383 soldiers. The point scores explain the exchange: almost every man leaving on SO 66 had 78 or more, almost every man arriving on SO 226 had fewer. Men with enough points went home. Men without them were transferred to units that stayed.

Each dot is one man, keyed to his Army serial number. These documents record just one relationship between any two of them — that they held the same military occupational specialty, and so did the same job — so that is what the lines mean. Men who worked alongside each other cluster together.
